AfricaBioLite

AfricaBP Genomic Gateway — genome browsing from a feature phone · Lead AI Developer, African BioGenome Project

Most genomic resources assume a broadband connection and a browser. AfricaBioLite lets a researcher, plant breeder or clinician query them by SMS, USSD or WhatsApp, from any phone, anywhere with a mobile signal.

A user sends a short command — GENE HUMAN BRAF, POPGEN HUMAN rs113488022, MAP CASSAVA 1:0‑50000 — or walks a USSD menu. The gateway queries Ensembl, UCSC, GTEx, EMBL‑EBI, NCBI and 1000 Genomes and returns a condensed text answer, an ASCII genome track for feature phones, or a compressed track image.

It covers genes, variants with ClinVar significance, sequence, orthologs, disease associations, tissue expression and allele frequencies across seven African 1000 Genomes populations with Fst differentiation, for 15 species from human to cassava, yam, cocoa and Plasmodium. Curated trait and drug‑resistance loci (e.g. kelch13, HBB, G6PD, APOL1) are shown only after Ensembl verifies them on the current assembly.

// engineering
  • One WSGI app serves every channel (USSD, SMS, WhatsApp Cloud API); stateless USSD sessions replay through a menu state machine, so it scales across gunicorn workers.
  • USSD never blocks on slow APIs: queries run on a Redis/RQ job queue, answering on‑screen when fast and by SMS with a reference number otherwise.
  • Neural image compression: a MobileNet encoder squeezes genome‑track images into a ~2 KB latent sent as a handful of SMS packets and decoded on the other end; runs on a Raspberry Pi with a USB GSM modem.
  • Offline‑first SQLite cache with per‑data‑type expiry and prefetch of key loci over Wi‑Fi.
  • Privacy and safety: phone numbers are keyed pseudonyms, never stored raw; results carry a clinical disclaimer.
  • Nine languages (English, Swahili, French, Hausa, Yoruba, Amharic, Twi, Arabic, Portuguese), each gated behind native‑speaker review, with checks that a translation can't break menu numbering or the 182‑character USSD limit.
